## Start with what Shopify already solves

Shopify handles the parts of running a webshop that are expensive to get wrong: PCI-compliant payments, checkout, hosting and uptime, security patching, and a plugin ecosystem for the features most stores need (reviews, subscriptions, loyalty, local payment methods like iDEAL). Building all of that yourself is a real software project, not a website, which is why "custom" almost never means custom payments and checkout too. It means a custom front end and custom logic layered on top of Shopify's commerce engine, or in rarer cases, a fully headless build.

That framing matters, because it changes the real question. It's not "Shopify or custom," it's "how much custom work does this specific store actually need, and on top of which foundation."

## When Shopify is the right call

Shopify is the right foundation for the large majority of stores, including ones that feel "too serious" for a template:

- **Standard retail logic.** Products, variants, collections, discounts, and a normal checkout flow, even for a large, catalog-heavy store.
- **You want to move fast.** A modified theme or a custom Shopify build still ships in weeks, not months, because you're not building payments, cart, or checkout from zero.
- **Multi-market selling.** Shopify Markets handles currencies, duties, and localised checkout across countries without a separate build per market.
- **A small internal team.** Shopify's app ecosystem and standard admin mean less custom system your own team has to learn and maintain after launch.

A well-designed, custom-built Shopify theme can look and feel completely bespoke. "Shopify" describes the engine, not the ceiling on the design.

## When a custom or headless build earns its cost

A fully custom or headless commerce build (Shopify Plus + a headless front end, or a platform like Medusa) starts making sense when the store's requirements genuinely don't fit Shopify's model, not just when the brand wants to feel exclusive:

- **Non-standard pricing or product logic:** real-time configurators, complex B2B pricing tiers, or highly custom bundling that fights against Shopify's product model instead of working with it.
- **Very high traffic or transaction volume** where checkout performance and infrastructure control become a genuine engineering concern, not a theoretical one.
- **Deep integration with existing systems:** a proprietary ERP, warehouse system, or CRM that a standard Shopify app can't talk to cleanly.
- **A front-end experience Shopify's theme architecture can't express:** heavily app-like interfaces, real-time features, or content structures that don't map to products and collections at all.

If none of those apply, "we need something fully custom" is usually a design ambition, not a platform requirement, and that ambition can almost always be built on Shopify instead.

## The cost and maintenance difference

A modified or custom Shopify theme is cheaper to build and cheaper to run: Shopify absorbs hosting, security patching, and PCI compliance, so your ongoing cost is mostly apps, theme updates, and content. A custom or headless build shifts that responsibility onto you: someone has to own hosting, security updates, and infrastructure monitoring indefinitely, which is a real, recurring cost most teams underestimate at the pitch stage. That's not a reason to avoid custom builds when they're genuinely warranted; it's a reason to budget for year two and three, not just launch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I migrate from a custom platform to Shopify later?

Yes, and it's a common move, usually driven by wanting to spend less time on infrastructure and more on the storefront and marketing. Product and order data migrates cleanly in most cases; the bigger work is usually rebuilding any custom logic that had no Shopify equivalent.

### Does Shopify limit design flexibility?

Not in practice. Shopify's theme architecture (Liquid, and newer section-based templating) supports fully custom front-end design. What you see as a generic "Shopify look" is almost always an unmodified stock theme, not a limitation of the platform itself.

### Is Shopify Plus the same as a headless build?

No. Shopify Plus is Shopify's enterprise tier: higher checkout limits, more automation, wholesale channels, still using Shopify's standard front end unless you deliberately pair it with a separate headless front end. You can be on Shopify Plus without being headless, and headless without being on Plus.

### What's the realistic timeline difference between the two?

A modified Shopify theme typically ships in 4–8 weeks. A fully custom or headless build is usually 3–6 months, because you're building storefront logic that Shopify would otherwise hand you for free.
